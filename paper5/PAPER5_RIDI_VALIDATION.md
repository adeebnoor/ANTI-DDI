# Superseded experimental validation — retained for provenance

The four-arm RIDI/classifier experiment formerly described in this file is **not valid as evidence for Anti-DDI**.

A post-analysis adversarial audit identified two primary design failures:

1. the Anti-DDI evidence payload was asymmetrically associated with the negative training class and acted as a surrogate label;
2. the Anti-DDI intervention was not applied to the held-out confirmed-interaction safety controls in the original safety comparison.

Additional testing showed that a generic Anti-DDI payload could suppress scores for confirmed interaction pairs and that one replication backend reached an all-tied score floor. Accordingly, the RIDI efficacy and safety claims from that experiment are withdrawn and are not used in the revised Communications Medicine resource/framework manuscript.

The underlying resource tables, audit findings, evidence tiers, structural-bias analyses and targeted human clinical-anchor table are separate from this failed experiment and remain available for independent evaluation.
